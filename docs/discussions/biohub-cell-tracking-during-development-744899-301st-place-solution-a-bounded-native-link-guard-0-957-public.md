I finished Biohub - Cell Tracking During Development in 301st place out of 4,017 teams, with a bronze medal, according to Kaggle's completion email. My selected notebook, Biohub Final D1 Bounded Native Guard, version 2, recorded a best public score of 0.957. I do not have a separately verified private score to report. My final change was a narrow postprocessing rule within an existing dual-model 3D detection and tracking pipeline. It combines pretrained UNet/Transformer predictions and spatial test-time views, with a separate DeepCenter 3D checkpoint used as an additional gate for selected gap and division decisions. The submission notebook performs inference only. The problem I targeted was a disagreement introduced by motion relinking: it can displace an original model link between two detections. D1 considers restoring only displaced native one-to-one links with learned probability ≥0.60, endpoint distance ≤5.5 µm and endpoints present before discarded-detection readmission. Raw division edges and conflicts involving readmitted nodes or division-like outgoing configurations are excluded. Restoration is bounded by min(256, ceil(0.002 × raw edge count)), with at least one slot when raw edges exist. Candidates are ordered deterministically by confidence and distance. A restoration may replace conflicting motion links, after which graph repair enforces the output topology. The rule does not add detections or globally relax linking. I checked it with a paired replay of the complete postprocessing and official scoring code on 12 fixed training movies, first reproducing the original graph hashes and metric columns. The combined development delta was +0.0011053, with +0.0010566 on four additional movies. All 12 leave-one-movie-out comparisons remained positive. Edge accounting changed by +3 true positives, −6 false positives and −3 false negatives; division counts were unchanged. These movies were exposed to training, so this is development evidence, not an independent unseen-embryo validation result. The public increase from earlier 0.955 submissions to 0.957 is a separate observation and does not, by itself, prove causality. The selected version completed a visible 20 min 20 s T4 × 2 run. Its CSV passed dataset coverage, schema, coordinate-bound and graph-topology checks, with no reported repair/deadline fallback. Those checks establish artifact validity, not biological correctness. I also tested several extensions that were negative, neutral or insufficiently supported and left them out of the final candidate. My write-up explains the guard, development replay, execution checks and failed attempts: [https://kaggle.com/competitions/biohub-cell-tracking-during-development/writeups/301st-place-bounded-native-links-for-3d-cell-tracI](https://kaggle.com/competitions/biohub-cell-tracking-during-development/writeups/301st-place-bounded-native-links-for-3d-cell-tracI) would welcome discussion of similarly conservative ways to reconcile learned associations with motion postprocessing, especially when annotations are sparse.
